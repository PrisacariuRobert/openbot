# OpenBot: a zero-budget launch that earns trust

Prepared 8 September 2026. This is an execution plan, not a promise of reach. No posts, messages, account changes or public releases were made as part of preparing it.

## The recommendation

Make OpenBot known for **a team you can shape, work you can inspect, and AI you can choose**. Lead with one useful result per post. Use the polished film to make people curious, then a real screen recording to answer “does it actually work?”

Start with Mac-using independent developers, technical freelancers and small-team builders who already use AI and can tolerate an early open-source beta. They can test it, explain what broke and contribute. “For everyone” is a longer-term product goal, not an effective first audience.

The first campaign should earn **five qualified pilot volunteers**, not chase a star count. A qualified volunteer has a compatible Mac, one specific task, time to report the outcome and understands provider costs/permissions. This is an internal target, not a forecast.

## Positioning people can repeat

**Category:** an open-source workspace for AI teammates.

**Short promise:** Your team. Your AI. Work you can inspect.

**Plain explanation:** OpenBot brings customizable AI teammates into a conversation-first app. Give them a task, choose a supported provider, review sensitive actions and inspect the work they return.

**Why it is interesting:** the combination of approachable teammates, inspectable work and an open codebase—not a claim that mascots or tool use are unique inventions.

**Where Grok Bot fits:** a useful reference when somebody asks. Say “I’m building an open-source alternative in the AI-teammate category.” Do not make every headline about a competitor. Do not claim parity, superiority or compatibility without a dated, reproducible comparison. Give upstream projects proper credit.

**Cost language:** Open-source software is not the same as unlimited free AI. Supported providers may charge or impose subscription limits; hardware, hosting and your time also have costs. This plan requires no ad spend, paid scheduler, purchased audience or paid launch service.

## Use two channels well

This channel choice is my recommendation for the initial audience, not a claim about guaranteed algorithmic reach.

| Channel | Job | Sustainable cadence | What belongs there |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Your personal LinkedIn | Explain the useful outcome to people and small teams | Two original posts/week | A real use case, a short video, what you learned building it |
| Your personal X account | Reach developers and open-source builders | Three posts/week, reusing the same two core stories | A concise clip, implementation detail or honest before/after |
| GitHub | Turn curiosity into understanding and contribution | One useful weekly update | Clear status, reproducible examples, bounded contribution requests |
| One relevant Reddit community | Get substantive feedback | At most one tailored post/week **if permitted** | A technical or user problem the community actually discusses |
| YouTube | A durable place for the film and later demos | One introduction, then one real demo/week if manageable | Searchable task-specific videos, not a daily feature montage |

Do not start a Discord, newsletter, TikTok account and Instagram account simultaneously. Add a channel only when the existing loop produces useful conversations without exhausting development time. If your existing network is stronger on a different channel, replace a primary channel rather than adding a sixth obligation.

Reddit is not a link-distribution service: disclose that you built OpenBot, answer questions, respect each community’s current rules and do not mass-post or send unsolicited promotional DMs. `r/opensource` and `r/SideProject` are **candidates to check**, not permission to post. Their rules pages did not expose enough detail through the research tool; inspect them manually before writing a community-specific post. Reddit’s [spam policy](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043504051-Spam) explicitly covers repeated manual as well as automated promotion.

## The content engine: one task, several useful pieces

Pick one real task each week. Record it once with sample data, then publish different aspects—not the same caption everywhere.

1. **Outcome, 15–30 seconds:** start with the finished artifact, show the request, show the check. One task and one next step.
2. **Mechanism, 30–60 seconds:** show why approval, review or model choice matters in that same task.
3. **Builder note:** one design or engineering decision, including the trade-off.
4. **Follow-up:** answer the best genuine question with evidence, including a failure if that is what happened.

Use the refined 64-second film once as the pinned introduction. It uses fictional, choreographed UI examples. Describe it as a **product preview** in the post. It should not be repackaged as a real execution benchmark. The strongest second video is an actual app recording, even if it is less cinematic.

### Three pilot stories to prove before advertising as working outcomes

| Story | A credible recording | What does not count as proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One request, one team answer | A bounded task, private consultation, one final response and the resulting file reopened | Three mascots moving or a model saying it consulted someone |
| A project change you can inspect | A tiny sample repo, requested change, actual diff, tests and scoped artifact/commit | A code-looking animation or invented “tests pass” badge |
| Repeat useful work | A routine configured, scheduled time reached, run recorded and outcome reopened | Saving a schedule without seeing a run |

The self-extending tool flow is an excellent fourth story **after** its approval, exact file effect, model selection and subsequent behavior are independently checked. Never use personal mail or cookies to get a better-looking demonstration.

## The first four weeks

This is a rolling schedule. “Launch” moves if the app is not ready; content does not override the release gate. Use the matching drafts in `SOCIAL_POSTS.md` and fill the tracker at the bottom of this document.

| Week | Monday | Wednesday | Friday | Product/community action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1: explain the idea | P1: founder introduction + refined film preview | P2: create your own teammate | P3: the problem with AI “done” messages | Ask responders for one concrete task; log objections |
| 2: earn belief | P4: one real result, only once recorded | P5: permission/review boundary | P6: a failure and what changed | Invite a small number of willing people to a safe pilot once ready |
| 3: show a pattern | P7: user/provider choice | P8: private team work, one answer | P9: focused contributor request | Fix the most common onboarding blocker; help a willing tester finish |
| 4: decide, don’t force | P10: consented pilot lesson | P11: beta announcement **only if ready** | P12: what changed because of feedback | Repeat the strongest story; defer broad launch if activation is weak |

Publish from your own perspective. On LinkedIn, explain the work and consequence; on X, keep the hook and one demonstration. Do not assume a magic posting time: try a time when you can answer for the next 30 minutes, then compare comparable posts. Do not claim links belong in comments because of an unverified algorithm theory; place one usable next step where readers can find it.

### Weekly time budget: approximately four hours

- 75 minutes: record one real task and cut a short demo, including a muted phone-size review.
- 45 minutes: adapt two stories into the week’s posts; reuse the footage.
- 20 minutes on three posting days: respond thoughtfully and offer one bounded next step.
- 20 minutes: contribute help in a relevant conversation without forcing a link.
- 40 minutes: help a willing tester or review metrics and decide the next task.

If you have only 90 minutes: one real demo, one founder post, two reply sessions. Keep the quality bar; reduce the number of channels.

## Measure usefulness, not applause

Use the free native post statistics available to your account, a local Markdown log and GitHub Insights. Do not add invasive product telemetry just for this campaign. GitHub exposes recent traffic, including visitors and full clones, in a rolling 14-day window; record a weekly snapshot and do not add overlapping rolling totals together. A clone is not an installation or a person. [GitHub traffic documentation](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/viewing-activity-and-data-for-your-repository/viewing-traffic-to-a-repository).

Track these separately:

- **Attention:** views/impressions where available, meaningful replies, visits. Platform view definitions differ; compare within a channel.
- **Intent:** people who describe a task and willingly ask to try it.
- **Activation:** a tester finishes one useful bounded task and can show the result.
- **Return:** a tester uses OpenBot again the next week, confirmed voluntarily.
- **Contribution:** a reproducible bug, docs correction, useful recipe or reviewed PR.

Initial working targets for the first four weeks: 10 substantive conversations → 5 willing pilots → 3 first useful tasks → 2 returning testers. These numbers are assumptions to guide effort, not promised conversions or industry benchmarks. Before safe installation is ready, evaluate conversations only.

**Decision rules:** after two weeks, if three task-specific posts receive no useful response, narrow the audience/task before increasing volume. If people visit but cannot explain OpenBot, simplify the first sentence and example. If they volunteer but cannot finish setup, stop broad promotion and fix onboarding. If the same task earns repeat use, make it the next campaign’s lead story. Do not manufacture testimonials to fill a gap.

## Recording and publishing checklist

- Begin with a useful action or result in the first two seconds. Make the story understandable muted; no voice is required.
- Keep one big readable action at a time. A 1920×1080 app window shrunk into a vertical canvas is not a mobile demo. For later Shorts/Reels, recompose the relevant conversation, not a blind center crop.
- Recommended working exports: master 1920×1080; feed demo 1080×1350; short vertical 1080×1920. These are creative presets, not universal platform requirements. Only the landscape master has been delivered in this pass.
- Use original or appropriately licensed music. Avoid Apple logos/music, unlicensed trending tracks, fabricated customer names and staged testimonials.
- Separate screen recording, accelerated recording and animated preview in the caption. State time compression when a task is sped up. Never imply the edit’s duration equals real task time.
- Test on a phone muted and then with sound. Check that controls, captions and platform overlays do not hide the result.
- The 64-second master fits X’s documented non-Premium duration allowance (140 seconds; check file size before uploading). YouTube classifies qualifying square/vertical videos up to three minutes as Shorts, so the landscape master is not automatically a Short. [X video guidance](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-videos), [YouTube Shorts guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en).
- Before announcing availability: clear install/uninstall instructions; current known issues; critical security fixes; supported provider tested; first task on a clean Mac; real download checksum/signing information; reachable issue support. Never bypass these for a calendar date.
- Before linking the website publicly: owner-approved public access, test it logged out, correct download status and one working CTA. Do not share the current private preview URL to strangers.

## First session you can run yourself

1. Watch the refined film and approve the actual export.
2. Adapt P1 in your own voice and post the film as a development preview on your strongest existing account. Use the public repository link; no download claim.
3. Stay available for replies. Ask interested people what specific task they would want help with; do not ask everyone to star or repost.
4. Turn the most repeated concrete problem into one safe real-app demo.
5. Record the outcome and repeat next week. Let the evidence drive the campaign.
