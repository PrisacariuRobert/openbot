## What the references change

The Air film begins in someone's working environment, cuts into the product, then turns active application windows into its graphic language. At roughly 9 seconds, hands and the keyboard become an extreme detail; around 12–24 seconds, applications, typography and the device's screen edge alternate in scale. Around 27–33 seconds, sticky notes and an editor occupy the frame. Near the end, it returns to the object and environment. These are approximate sample times, not an exact shot list.

The Vision Pro opening gives digital surfaces a physical context: the person, their gaze and gesture explain the experience. The lesson is not to put OpenBot in an artificial headset commercial. It is to make every camera move follow an interaction and its consequence.

Our earlier cuts kept resetting the viewer: headline, floating rectangle, next headline. Changing the easing or adding more zoom did not change that structure. Generic synthesized loops also supplied motion without a meaningful musical arc.

## Direction: built around you

The short test uses one differentiating idea: a teammate can propose building the tool it is missing. The visible sequence stays attached to one request. The narrator carries the explanation, leaving the screen free to demonstrate it.

- Begin close to an original mascot, then establish the small team. No opening feature list.
- Enter one request. Move with the conversation into the proposal.
- Cut closer for the plan, coding-model preference and approval. Let the approval land without a musical hit covering the voice.
- Follow the same task into a tool file. Finish in Files, where ownership is tangible.
- Use just one closing brand line. Do not burn the voice into giant matching sentence captions.

This is a staged motion/voice direction test, not a screen recording or proof of an autonomous tool build. It intentionally omits the rest of the feature catalog; the full film should only be rebuilt after this direction works.

## Voice and sound

The narrator is the same locally generated, built-in Aiden used by Robert's `GitHub/term` pipeline. The direction is warm and conversational rather than forensic/documentary. No Apple narrator or other identifiable person's voice was cloned. One sample was generated with offline model weights, then transcribed locally; human listening approval is still needed before batch production.

The test's original sound bed is a sparse plucked-string texture with soft percussion, a deliberate gap around approval, and a short resolving phrase. It is a sound-direction sketch, not a substitute for a professionally selected/composed final soundtrack. No Apple music, paid music service, or unlicensed stock is used. The next full-film pass needs a chosen musical identity, not another interchangeable background loop.
